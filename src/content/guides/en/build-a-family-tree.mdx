A family tree is more than a chart. It is a family's memory: your great-great-grandfather's name, the
year the family moved cities, who your mother's cousin married. If no one writes these down while the
older generation is still here to tell them, they are gone.

This guide walks through building a tree you can trust: collecting the information, recording it in
an [online family tree](/register), and turning it into something you can print and share.

## Step 1: Start with yourself, not the oldest ancestor

The common mistake is to start with the most distant name anyone remembers. Work the other way:
start with **yourself** and go back one generation at a time.

You know the near generations with certainty — your parents, your siblings, their dates. Every
generation back, the information gets thinner and less sure. Starting from the bottom means every new
generation connects to one you already know is right.

A practical order:

1. You, your spouse and your children
2. Your parents and your siblings
3. All four grandparents
4. Your aunts and uncles, and their children
5. Earlier generations, gradually

## Step 2: Collect the information

For each person, ask for and note down as much of this as you can. You will not have all of it, and
that is fine — anything you have is worth keeping:

- **First and family name** — record married women under their birth family name; it makes their
  own family much easier to trace later.
- **Dates of birth and death** — even an approximate year helps.
- **Places of birth and death** — a town or a village.
- **Parents** — the most important item, because it is what links the tree together.
- **Marriages** — to whom, from when, and a divorce date if there was one.
- **Notes** — occupation, a story, anything worth keeping.

### Who to ask

Your best source is the **older generation**. An hour with a grandparent usually yields more than
any document. A few tips:

- Ask open questions: "Tell me about your brothers and sisters" gets more than "How many siblings did
  you have?"
- If they agree, record the conversation. You will hear details later that you missed in the moment.
- Look through old photographs together. Every photo brings back a name and a story.

### Which documents help

Birth and marriage certificates, family bibles or notebooks, gravestones and old letters are all good
sources. When a date comes from a document, write in that person's notes where it came from.

## Step 3: Build the tree

Now record it. [Sign up](/register) for the family tree app — your account comes with two sample
trees, one in English and one in Persian, so you can see what a complete tree looks like. Then create
a tree for your own family and give it a name, such as "The Karimi Family".

### Add the first person

Use **Add person** to record the first person. A first name and a gender are required; every other
field is optional, and you can fill it in whenever you learn more.

**Dates can be entered in the Gregorian or the Persian (Jalali) calendar.** If you do not know a date,
leave it empty — a wrong guess is worse than a blank.

### Add relatives from any person

Select anyone on the tree and a panel opens with an **Add relatives** section: spouse, child, father
and mother. This is the fastest way to build, because the relationship is recorded at the same moment
and you never have to link people by hand afterwards.

- **Spouse:** adding a spouse asks for the marriage date. Marriages are recorded separately, so
  someone who married more than once has each marriage on its own.
- **Child:** the child is linked to both parents.
- **Father and mother:** to add the generation above someone.

### Record the right kind of parent

Not every parent is a birth parent. When you record a parent you can choose **biological**,
**adoptive** or **step**. That keeps the tree accurate and shows blended families as they really are.

The app also stops a few common mistakes on its own: no one can be their own ancestor, no one has more
than two biological parents, and a parent and child must belong to the same tree.

## Step 4: Got a long list? Use Excel

If you already have dozens of people in a spreadsheet, you do not need to enter them one by one. You
can bring them in from **Excel**: download the sample file, fill it in, and upload it with **Import
from Excel**. Before anything is saved you get a preview showing who is new and who will be updated.

The full format is in the [family tree Excel template](/guides/family-tree-excel-template) guide.

## Step 5: See how people are related

Once a few generations are in, the good part begins. Pick two people and use **Find relation**. The app
shows the closest path between them on the tree and names the relationship — "paternal uncle's son",
"maternal uncle's wife". If two people are related in more than one way, it offers the other paths
too. **Male-line path** finds a route that only passes through the men of the family.

If you just want to know what a relation is called, the [kinship name finder](/kinship) answers
without an account. And the [family relationship names](/guides/family-relationship-names) guide
explains the names themselves, in English and Persian.

## Step 6: Export and share

A tree that lives in one account is only half as useful.

- **Image and PDF:** **Export** shows a preview of the tree, then saves a PNG image or a PDF. For
  printing a large tree, the PDF can split it across several pages. You can change the colours and
  style too.
- **A person's poster:** for anyone, you can save an **info card** that lists their descendants
  generation by generation — a nice gift for a family gathering.
- **Excel:** **Export to Excel** gives you a copy of the whole tree that you can edit and import again.
- **Share with relatives:** invite other family members to the tree by their username and choose what
  each of them may do — only view, or add and edit. Everyone can then fill in the branch they know best.

## A few habits that keep a tree trustworthy

- **Keep guesses apart from facts.** If you are not sure, say so in the notes: "probably", or "according
  to Aunt Mina".
- **Do not record the same person twice.** Search the tree before adding someone, especially in
  families where the same names come back every generation.
- **Respect the living.** Trees in the app are private: only the members you invite can see them.
- **Build it gradually.** It does not have to be finished in a day. Every conversation with an older
  relative adds a few new branches.

The sooner you start a family tree, the more of it you can save. Start today, with yourself and your
parents.
